# Market brief: Indian consumer electricals, FY26 (April 2025 – March 2026)

Orvane Electricals is fictional and its data is synthetic. This brief compares it with what listed Indian companies reported for the same financial year, so the planted problems can be checked against what really happened in the industry. Figures come from company earnings calls and press reports of their results (sources at the end). Definitions differ between companies (contribution margin, EBITDA, segment boundaries), so the comparisons are directional, not like for like.

Orvane is sized like a mid-sized player: about half of V-Guard and a tenth of Havells.

## 2. What happened in the industry, and how Orvane compares

### Copper inflation was larger and longer than Orvane's
- **Industry:** copper rose more than 60% on MCX during calendar 2025 [5] and reached a record US$13,042 per tonne on the LME on 6 January 2026, on mine disruptions in Chile and Indonesia, US tariff stockpiling and forecast deficits [6].
- **Orvane:** copper rises 18.1% from April to September (₹780 → ₹921 per kg) and then eases. Wire gross margin falls from 20.1% to 8.0% in September and recovers to 17.3% after a single 12% price increase in October.
- **What the comparison says:** Orvane's copper shock is a milder version of the real one. Real companies did not reprice once. Havells described "calibrated price increases" of 5–20% across ranges [7]. V-Guard said about 75% of the needed increases had landed by May 2026, with the rest to come as high-cost stock arrived [3]. The recommendation in this project (a price-review trigger when copper moves more than 5%) matches how the industry actually had to respond: repeatedly, not once.

### Wire value grew faster than volume
- **Industry:** Havells reported 6% volume growth and 14% value growth in cables & wires in Q4, so about 8% came from price [7]. In Q2 it noted that wire volume grew more slowly than value because copper rose [8]. Polycab's wires & cables grew 33% for the year [1].
- **Orvane:** wire net sales rise from ₹61 crore a month in April to ₹96 crore in March, with units up about 40%, so price explains the rest. This is the same pattern, though Orvane has no inventory gains. Havells said its Q2 and Q4 cable margins were helped by inventory gains on copper [7][8], which a standard-cost model like Orvane's does not show.
- **Margins:** Havells guides a 15–16% contribution margin for cables & wires [8]. Orvane's wire gross margin of 17–20% in normal months is in the same range.

### Fans had a hard year; BLDC is where the market is moving
- **Industry:**
  - Havells' fans fell mid-single digits in Q2 and again in value in Q4. It blamed a shorter summer, rain into July, and a Q3 push ahead of new BEE energy-efficiency norms [7][8].
  - V-Guard's fans also declined in Q4. Its channel stocked up ahead of the December 2025 star-rating change, and BLDC is about 40% of its ceiling fan sales [3].
  - Crompton said its BLDC range was growing over 30% and that March 2026 brought record fan volumes [4].
- **Orvane:** the planted problem is a cheaper competitor BLDC fan taking premium share in the West from August (premium share 18% → 9%), seen first in the field notes.
- **What the comparison says:** the BLDC threat is realistic. Two things Orvane's data leaves out are worth flagging in a real review:
  - **Weather:** a late or short summer moves fan sales as much as competition does.
  - **Regulation:** the star-rating change pulled sales into Q3 through channel stocking. That would make Q4 look weak even with no loss of share.

### Water heaters grew
- **Industry:**
  - V-Guard said water heaters "grew strongly" in Q4 [3].
  - Crompton reported double-digit growth, and is preparing for BEE water heater norms from July 2026 [4].
  - Havells said water heaters "grew well" in Q2 [8].
- **Orvane:** demand for water heaters in the North was higher than Haridwar could make in winter, so 15,812 units went unshipped and ₹1.09 crore of extra freight was paid to supply from Hosur.
- **What the comparison says:** this is the category where capacity matters. In a growing market, supply that can't keep up loses sales that may not come back. That supports the business case's option C (fix the Haridwar downtime and pre-build only the rest) and argues against simply accepting the shortfall.

### Working capital and channels
- Havells' receivables fell from ₹1,254 crore to ₹782 crore in FY26, about 13 debtor days, through channel financing rather than changed terms [7]. V-Guard said about 35% of its business uses channel finance [3].
- Havells said distribution channels are over half its sales, and that e-commerce and modern retail pick up in Q4 [8].
- Orvane models distributor, modern retail, e-commerce and institutional channels, but not receivables or channel finance. Payment-terms notes (148 of 1,200) are its only signal on credit. A real COO review would add debtor days by channel.

## 3. What this changes in the project's recommendations

| Project finding | Industry evidence | Effect on the recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Copper hit wire margin; one price increase recovered it | Copper up over 60% in 2025; companies repriced several times [5][7][3] | Keep the copper trigger, but plan for several price rounds a year, not one |
| West premium fans lost share to BLDC | BLDC about 40% of V-Guard ceiling fans; Crompton BLDC +30% [3][4] | Keep the premium BLDC review; before blaming competition alone, check weather and the star-rating change |
| North water heater shortfall in winter | Water heaters growing at several companies [3][4][8] | Strengthens option C: lost winter sales are a bigger cost in a growing category |
| E-commerce has the lowest contribution after freight | E-commerce and modern retail pick up in Q4 [8] | Keep the freight renegotiation; e-commerce volumes are seasonal, so judge freight rates on a full year |
| Billing vs ledger cut-off in December | Not covered by the sources | No change |

## 4. Limits of this brief
- It is based on earnings call transcripts and press summaries of results, not full annual reports. Some figures (Crompton's full-year revenue, Havells' growth by segment) were not in the sources used and are left out rather than estimated.
- Orvane's numbers are synthetic. The point of the comparison is whether the planted problems behave like real ones, not to rank Orvane against real companies.
- Copper prices are quoted from press reports of LME and MCX moves, not from an exchange data series.
